# Peer-review approach: knowledge artifacts

Loaded by `/peerreview` for study repos generated by knowledge-skills: both
`context/sources.md` and `context/sources_zh.md`, plus at least two of
`concept_graph.md`, `critiques.md`, `deep_dive.md`, and `summary_zh.md`.
The source indexes and their snapshots are the factual ground truth; when
available, `~/personal/knowledge-skills/CLAUDE.md` and the producing command
files are the artifact-format contract. This profile may co-match
`source-rendered`; apply that guard to committed PNGs.

The dominant defect class is **unsupported claims propagated consistently
across multiple polished artifacts**. Internal agreement is weak evidence when
all outputs share one drafting pass, so review source→claim before
artifact→artifact consistency.

## Brief the PEER with these lenses

### Lens 1 — Citation semantics, not citation presence

For every numeric, named-entity, security-sensitive, or quoted claim—and a
risk-ranked sample of the rest—open the cited committed snapshot and verify it
asserts the specific claim. Topic adjacency is not support. A verbatim quote
must match character-for-character. Claims extending beyond the snapshot use
`[inference]`; fetch-blocked primaries do not become evidence through an
uncited secondary.

### Lens 2 — Source and reference closure

Run the validator below, then inspect exceptions rather than weakening it.
Every used `[E#]`/`[Z#]` resolves to a source entry, every citing artifact's
References section equals its body citation set, and committed primary
snapshots exist. Supplementary snapshots are intentionally gitignored and may
be absent after a fresh clone; do not require them. Check source language
classification (`E` = non-Chinese, `Z` = Chinese) and stable IDs without
renumbering.

### Lens 3 — Ground truth → deliverable completeness

Derive the expected artifact set from the active charter and producer contract;
each expected file must be substantive, not a placeholder. Then sweep source
entries in both language pools and confirm their unique mechanisms, caveats,
and contradictory evidence appear somewhere appropriate. Artifact→source
citation checks cannot detect an omitted source or an English-only synthesis
that silently ignores the Chinese pool.

### Lens 4 — Cross-artifact consistency after source verification

Once claims are source-settled, compare diagrams, concept graph, critique, deep
dive, and Chinese synthesis for incompatible terminology, directionality,
numbers, scope, or confidence. A later synthesis must not resurrect a claim
that the critique demoted. Different emphasis or Chinese phrasing is not drift;
contradictory factual commitments are.

### Lens 5 — Concept-graph integrity

Require no orphan nodes; every edge has a source ID or `[inference]`; the edge
table and Mermaid graph are exact `(from, to, relation)` multisets; node layers
and edge relations belong to the declared schemas; and every node appears once
in the learning tour. Independently verify edge direction and type against its
snapshot—the parity gate catches omissions/drift, not a consistently backwards claim.

### Lens 6 — Diagram syntax, rendering, and factual fidelity

Validate every Mermaid block, not only its type prefix; inspect rendered output
for clipped/overlapping labels and literal `<br/>` edge text. For drawio and
Python-diagrams sources with committed PNGs, use the `source-rendered` clean
re-render/diff guard and read the PNG visually. Finally re-derive arrows,
component names, sequence order, and boundaries from the snapshots; a clean
render can still communicate a false architecture.

## Verification-gate amendments

Run from the reviewed repo root every round:

```sh
python3 ~/personal/peerreview-skills/scripts/validate-knowledge-artifacts.py .
```

The script owns deterministic source-ID/reference closure, primary-snapshot
existence, concept-graph orphan/citation/tour and table↔Mermaid edge-parity checks,
Mermaid fence/type checks, and the known `<br/>` edge-label failure. The HOST additionally runs a pinned,
o-network Mermaid parser when available and applies the main skill's
`source-rendered` guard to committed images. Semantic citation support,
translation quality, visual legibility, and technical truth remain judgment.

## What NOT to flag

- `[inference]` when the prose clearly distinguishes synthesis from sourced fact.
- Missing local `context/originals-supplementary/` after clone; it is gitignored.
- An empty Chinese pool explicitly recorded by the source-gathering contract.
- Different organization or emphasis between English and Chinese synthesis.
- Low-level visual taste that does not impair meaning or legibility.

## Forecast hint

Citation-heavy multi-artifact repos usually need 2–4 rounds: first settle
source fidelity, then sweep propagation and visual/graph surfaces. Do not cap
the loop; a single wrong source claim can require a family-wide correction.
